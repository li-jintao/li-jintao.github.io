Selected Work

I work at the intersection of software engineering, applied ML, and supply chain operations. Much of my work is forward-deployed in practice: I work directly with planners and operational teams to understand a workflow, build a solution, deploy it to the cloud, and improve it based on how people actually use it.

My strongest hands-on delivery experience is on Google Cloud, with additional experience in AWS-based environments. These examples focus on engineering approaches and supply chain challenges without disclosing proprietary rules or internal data.

1. Production forecasting for container flows

Problem: Container planning requires weekly forecasts of equipment movements and returns across ports, depots, equipment types, and customer flows. Operational data can be sparse or inconsistent, so forecasts must fit the cadence and constraints of planning teams.

Contribution:

  • Developed Python and SQL forecasting workflows with historical features, sparse-data fallback logic, model evaluation, and recurring output generation.
  • Connected models with booking and container movement data to support repositioning and return planning.
  • Worked with regional operations and product stakeholders to review exceptions, validate model behavior, and refine outputs for practical use.

Engineering & deployment: Built and maintained cloud-based processing with BigQuery, Cloud Run, containerized Python workloads, and scheduled orchestration, with attention to validation, logging, testing, and repeatable releases.

Outcome: Improved container movement forecast accuracy and delivered recurring decision-support outputs for global shipping operations.

2. Cost optimization and supply chain decision systems

Problem: Equipment repositioning and transportation decisions involve location constraints, historical flows, tariffs, and competing cost and service objectives. Planners need transparent scenario comparisons reflecting how the network operates.

Contribution:

  • Formulated operational questions as measurable optimization objectives, constraints, and cost rules.
  • Worked with movement histories, location hierarchies, equipment attributes, and transportation or storage cost inputs to build analytical workflows and proofs of concept.
  • Designed fallbacks, validation checks, and interpretable outputs to help business users investigate exceptions and compare options.

Engineering & deployment: Implemented data preparation and calculation workflows using Python, SQL, and cloud data services, connecting source data, business logic, and consumable results. Focused on maintainable components that can evolve from analysis into operational tools.

Outcome: Supported operating-cost reduction and more consistent evaluation of supply chain planning options.

3. Forward-deployed cloud engineering and internal tools

Problem: Operational teams need usable, reliable solutions that fit existing processes, without depending on manual exports, disconnected scripts, or ongoing interpretation by an engineer.

Contribution:

  • Took internal tools from user discovery and prototyping through data integration, implementation, deployment, and iteration.
  • Built Python data services and workflows, connected internal datasets and APIs, and developed ways to share forecasting and optimization outputs with users.
  • Owned decisions around runtime design, packaging, deployment, error handling, testing, and ongoing maintenance while working with IT, product, and operations teams.

Engineering & deployment: Hands-on with GCP (BigQuery, Cloud Run, workflow orchestration), AWS-based platforms, Python, SQL, Git, containers, CI/CD practices, and cloud-native development. Experience spans modeling, data systems, and operational delivery.

Outcome: Delivered internal analytics and decision-support tools end to end, reducing the gap between business problems and practical applications.

How I Work

Discover the workflow. Work directly with decision-makers to define the problem, operational constraints, inputs, and measures of success.

Build the full solution. Move between data engineering, modeling, APIs, cloud services, and user-facing delivery as needed.

Ship and improve. Validate against real cases, automate repeatable execution, monitor failures, and iterate with users after deployment.
